# Day 02 – Events

**Core idea:** Photograph the temporary organization of attention — the moment a group of people share a single focus.

## Why this exercise matters
Ordinary street is diffuse. An event creates hierarchy: a clear center and a ring of reaction. Learning to work both the center and the edges builds anticipation, spatial control, and the ability to read collective body language. These skills transfer directly to denser street work and to approached portraits (you learn to see the whole social situation, not just the face).

## Shooting brief
Any small public event or moment of shared attention (street performer, argument, tight queue, celebration, minor incident, political gathering). 45–70 minutes. Capture both the focus and the reaction around it. Minimum 6 clear event frames. Volume 40–70.

## Technical & practical guidance
- Settings: AF-C with subject tracking if available, continuous low or single frame. f/4–5.6, shutter 1/320s or faster.
- Focusing: Continuous on the central subject or the strongest reacting face in the ring.
- Stance: Start on the perimeter. Move in only when the geometry supports a stronger frame. Keep elbows in; move like you belong.
- Light: Use whatever the event gives. Backlight can separate the center from the crowd. Avoid shooting into pure silhouette unless that is the point.
- Distance: 2–6 meters. Wider end of the 24-50mm for context; longer end to isolate reaction.

Primarily candid. Brief engagement is acceptable only if it does not destroy the event dynamic.

## Common failure modes
- Photographing only the center and missing the reaction.
- Arriving after the peak and shooting leftovers.
- Including large empty foreground that dilutes the energy.
- Getting so close that you become part of the event and change it.

## Good vs excellent
**Good:** You show that something is happening and people are reacting.  
**Excellent:** The reaction carries equal or greater weight than the event itself. Spatial layers are clear. The frame has tension between center and edge.
