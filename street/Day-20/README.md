# Day 20 – Ethics

**Core idea:** Shoot with a clear, personal, practical code. Make pictures you would be prepared to show the subjects or defend without rationalization.

## Why this exercise matters
Technical skill without ethical clarity produces work that eventually feels extractive or hollow. This day forces the code into practice under real conditions rather than leaving it as abstract principle. It is the final filter for all the previous days.

## Shooting brief
Normal street work with heightened awareness of consent signals, dignity, power dynamics, and potential harm. Actively avoid situations that feel extractive or voyeuristic. If someone notices and shows discomfort, stop, delete if requested, or engage respectfully. Make at least 6 frames you would be willing to show the subjects themselves. Volume 40–60.

## Technical & practical guidance
No special technical settings. The constraint is behavioral and ethical.

For approached street portraits: clear, brief, respectful communication.

For candid work: continuous reading of the situation. Distance and stance should never feel predatory.

Your body language and speed of movement communicate as much as the camera.

## Common failure modes
- Abstract principles that disappear the moment a strong picture appears.
- Rationalizing after the fact (“they didn’t see me,” “it’s public,” “the picture is good enough to justify it”).
- Ignoring clear discomfort.
- Treating ethics as a one-day exercise instead of a permanent operating system.

## Good vs excellent
**Good:** No obvious ethical breaches in the day’s work.  
**Excellent:** The pictures are strong and you have a clear, personal rule set that you actually follow under pressure. You know exactly what you will and will not do, and the work reflects that clarity.
