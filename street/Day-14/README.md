# Day 14 – Empty

**Core idea:** Photograph urban emptiness and quiet so that absence itself becomes the subject.

## Why this exercise matters
Most street photographers only feel competent when people are present. Empty frames force reliance on light, geometry, atmosphere, and precise framing. This builds formal strength and the ability to make a complete picture from limited content — a skill that improves every other day.

## Shooting brief
Early morning streets, closed shops, vacant lots, quiet alleys, deserted platforms, empty benches. Make the emptiness feel intentional and atmospheric. Minimum 6 frames that successfully convey emptiness or solitude. Volume 30–50. Light and geometry must carry the picture.

## Technical & practical guidance
- Settings: Can use lower shutter speeds if the scene is completely static.
- Focusing: On the strongest geometric or light plane.
- Stance: Precise framing. Edges and corners matter more when content is sparse.
- Light: Directional light or interesting sky is often essential. Flat light makes emptiness look merely empty.
- Distance: Often wider to emphasize the scale of the emptiness.

No people, or only distant and clearly secondary.

## Common failure modes
- Boring emptiness (just nothing happening).
- Weak light.
- Poor edge control and messy corners.
- Including small distractions that break the sense of absence.

## Good vs excellent
**Good:** The place is empty.  
**Excellent:** The emptiness has mood, the frame feels complete and deliberate, and the picture would be weaker if a person were added.
