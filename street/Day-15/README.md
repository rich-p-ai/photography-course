# Day 15 – Objects

**Core idea:** Treat ordinary urban objects as the primary subject with the same seriousness you give people.

## Why this exercise matters
Objects carry atmosphere, social information, humor, loneliness, and graphic power. Learning to see them as subjects expands your range and improves still-life seeing on the street. It also trains you to use light and context rather than relying on human expression.

## Shooting brief
Chairs, signs, barriers, discarded items, street furniture, temporary structures, objects left behind. Minimum 8 frames where an object or small group of objects is clearly the subject. Volume 40–60. Context and light matter — avoid pure product shots.

## Technical & practical guidance
- Settings: f/4–8 depending on desired depth.
- Focusing: Precise on the object.
- Stance: Get closer when it strengthens form and presence. Watch backgrounds and edges carefully.
- Light: Side light or hard light reveals texture, volume, and character.
- Distance: Often closer than typical people work.

Human presence only if it serves the object story.

## Common failure modes
- Product-style shots with no atmosphere or context.
- Weak light that makes the object look ordinary.
- Cluttered surroundings that compete.
- Choosing objects with no visual or emotional charge.

## Good vs excellent
**Good:** An object is photographed cleanly.  
**Excellent:** The object feels charged — lonely, funny, graphic, or socially revealing — and the frame supports that reading through light, context, and precise framing.
