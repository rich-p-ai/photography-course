# Day 17 – Children

**Core idea:** Photograph children in public space with genuine interest in their world, strict dignity, and clear ethical limits.

## Why this exercise matters
Children are a high-stakes subject. Doing this well builds emotional intelligence, timing for unpredictable movement, lower-angle seeing, and personal ethical boundaries that apply to all street portraiture. It forces you to decide in real time what is acceptable and what is not.

## Shooting brief
Children playing, walking with adults, or occupying public space in interesting ways. Prefer wider context and interactions over tight facial close-ups of strangers’ children. Minimum 6 frames. If a parent notices and shows discomfort, move on immediately. Volume 40–60.

## Technical & practical guidance
- Settings: Fast shutter (often 1/500s or higher), AF-C with tracking.
- Focusing: Continuous on the child.
- Stance: Lower angles frequently work better and feel less predatory.
- Light: Available light. Avoid harsh overhead sun that creates ugly shadows on faces.
- Distance: Respectful. Wider context is both safer and often stronger.

Primarily candid. Approached only with clear parental comfort and only if it serves a stronger picture.

## Common failure modes
- Exploitative or cute framing.
- Tight faces of strangers’ children without meaningful context.
- Ignoring parental signals.
- Making the child look vulnerable or performed for the camera.

## Good vs excellent
**Good:** A decent, non-exploitative picture of a child.  
**Excellent:** The frame shows the child’s world or energy with dignity and the same formal strength you would demand of any adult subject. Ethics and craft are both visible.
