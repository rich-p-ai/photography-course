# Day 04 – Lining Up

**Core idea:** Align separate elements in the frame so they form one deliberate graphic or narrative relationship.

## Why this exercise matters
This is the skill that turns a picture containing interesting things into a constructed picture. Precise alignment is what makes certain frames feel inevitable rather than lucky. It is also one of the fastest ways to improve composition under pressure.

## Shooting brief
Busy or semi-busy public space. Wait for or create clean alignments (person to architecture, shadow to figure, gesture to sign, two people forming a single shape, etc.). Minimum 6 frames where the alignment is obvious and intentional. Both vertical and horizontal. Volume 40–70.

## Technical & practical guidance
- Settings: f/5.6–8. Grid overlay on. Shutter fast enough for the subject.
- Focusing: On the plane where the critical alignment happens.
- Stance: Micro-movements of body and camera matter more than zoom. A 10 cm shift can complete or destroy an alignment.
- Light: Side light or hard light makes alignments graphic and readable.
- Distance: Usually 4–10 meters so elements sit in clear relationship without extreme perspective distortion.

Candid. Patience is the primary technique.

## Common failure modes
- Accepting near-misses.
- Including distractions that break the alignment.
- Moving too much instead of waiting for the second element.
- Over-zooming and losing the relationship between elements.

## Good vs excellent
**Good:** The alignment is visible.  
**Excellent:** The alignment is exact, the rest of the frame supports it without clutter, and the picture collapses if any key element is removed.
