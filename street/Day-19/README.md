# Day 19 – Vertical / Horizontal

**Core idea:** Make orientation an active decision rather than a camera default. Shoot half the session only vertical, half only horizontal, and compare.

## Why this exercise matters
Default horizontal is lazy. Orientation changes emphasis, meaning, and the relationships inside the frame. Vertical often isolates and stacks; horizontal often relates and contextualizes. Making the choice deliberate improves every frame you take afterward.

## Shooting brief
First half of the session: only vertical (portrait orientation). Second half: only horizontal (landscape orientation). Revisit the same scenes in both orientations when possible and compare. Minimum 6 strong frames in each orientation. Volume 50–80.

## Technical & practical guidance
- Settings: Standard street settings for the conditions.
- Focusing and light: Unchanged by orientation.
- Stance: Recompose fully for the new orientation. Do not simply rotate the camera and crop later.
- Distance: Adjust as needed for the new frame shape.

Both candid street and any street portraits.

## Common failure modes
- Simply rotating the camera without rethinking the composition.
- Weak verticals that are just cropped horizontals.
- Ignoring how orientation changes the weight of elements.
- Defaulting back to horizontal the moment the exercise feels restrictive.

## Good vs excellent
**Good:** Usable frames in both orientations.  
**Excellent:** Certain scenes clearly belong to one orientation, and you can articulate exactly why. Orientation has become a deliberate compositional tool.

## Other sources
Compare vertical and horizontal frames by Cartier-Bresson, Winogrand, and Meyerowitz of similar subject matter. Notice how the choice changes meaning.
