# Day 09 – Blurred

**Core idea:** Use intentional motion blur (panning or static camera with moving subjects) as the primary quality of the picture.

## Why this exercise matters
Sharpness is a default, not a requirement. Controlled blur can carry energy, atmosphere, and time in ways that sharp frames cannot. Learning when and how to use it expands your visual vocabulary and prevents you from rejecting pictures simply because they are not tack-sharp.

## Shooting brief
Shutter speeds roughly 1/15s to 1/4s (experiment). Two methods: (a) panning with a moving subject, (b) static camera with moving people or traffic creating streaks. Minimum 6 frames where blur is clearly intentional and successful. Volume 40–70. Cull pure failures on the camera.

## Technical & practical guidance
- Settings: Shutter priority or full manual. Start around 1/15s and adjust. Raise ISO if needed to keep aperture in a usable range (f/4–8).
- Focusing: Pre-focus on the subject plane or use continuous.
- Stance: Brace against a post, railing, or wall for static-camera blur. For panning, smooth horizontal movement from the hips, not the arms.
- Light: Overcast or lower light helps keep shutter slow without extreme filters.
- Distance: Medium for panning; any for static streaks.

## Common failure modes
- Blur that looks like a mistake rather than a decision.
- No clear subject still readable inside the blur.
- Camera shake that is uncontrolled rather than directional.
- Accepting frames where everything is equally soft.

## Good vs excellent
**Good:** Intentional blur is visible.  
**Excellent:** The blur has clear direction and energy, and a subject still reads inside it. The picture feels more alive than a sharp version of the same scene would.
