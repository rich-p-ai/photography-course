# Day 13 – Doubles

**Core idea:** Find or wait for visual pairs — similar shapes, postures, gestures, or forms that echo each other inside the frame.

## Why this exercise matters
Visual rhyme creates instant structure and often humor or tension. It trains coincidence recognition and the ability to hold a frame open until the second element arrives. This is central to Elliott Erwitt’s best work and to Matt Stuart’s strongest coincidences.

## Shooting brief
Pairs: two people with similar posture, matching shapes between architecture and human form, repeated gestures, twin objects. The double must feel intentional. Minimum 6 clear double/pair compositions. Volume 40–70. Timing is usually critical when people are involved.

## Technical & practical guidance
- Settings: Shutter fast enough for gesture.
- Focusing: On the plane of the critical pair.
- Stance: Position so both elements sit in clear relationship. Longer end of the zoom often helps compress and emphasize the rhyme.
- Light: Even or side light so both elements read with similar weight.
- Distance: Medium working distance is common.

Candid. Patience for the second element to appear or align is the method.

## Common failure modes
- Accepting near-misses as doubles.
- Pairs that are too dissimilar to create real rhyme.
- Clutter that hides the relationship.
- Shooting before the second element has fully arrived.

## Good vs excellent
**Good:** Two similar things are present.  
**Excellent:** The echo is precise, the rest of the frame supports the relationship without distraction, and the double creates tension or humor that elevates the picture.
