# Day 10 – Layers

**Core idea:** Build frames with clear foreground, midground, and background that interact without collapsing into clutter.

## Why this exercise matters
Depth and controlled complexity separate competent street pictures from flat ones. Layers create visual richness and allow multiple points of interest to coexist. This is a core skill in the work of Alex Webb, Joel Meyerowitz, and the denser frames of Winogrand. It also improves street portraits by teaching you to place a person inside a meaningful environment rather than against a clean backdrop.

## Shooting brief
Actively seek three-plane compositions. Look for overlapping people, architecture, reflections, or foliage that create depth. Minimum 6 frames with clear, readable layers. Volume 40–70. Test both the wider and longer ends of the 24-50mm.

## Technical & practical guidance
- Settings: f/8–11 preferred for multi-plane sharpness on APS-C.
- Focusing: Usually on the midground or the strongest subject plane.
- Stance: Move your feet to control overlaps and avoid merges (elements that touch and flatten the space).
- Light: Side light or backlight helps separate planes. Flat light kills layer separation.
- Distance: Wider end exaggerates depth; longer end compresses layers. Both are valid — choose deliberately.

Candid. People are often the connecting tissue between planes.

## Common failure modes
- Uncontrolled merges that flatten the image.
- Too many planes with no hierarchy.
- Flat light that removes separation.
- Focusing on the wrong plane so the key relationship is soft.

## Good vs excellent
**Good:** Three planes are present.  
**Excellent:** The planes interact (through gesture, gaze, graphic echo, or light) and the picture remains clear and organized rather than busy.
