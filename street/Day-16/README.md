# Day 16 – Graphic

**Core idea:** Reduce the street to pure formal elements — shape, line, contrast, color block, geometry — so the picture works primarily through visual structure.

## Why this exercise matters
Formal strength is the foundation underneath narrative. A picture with strong graphic bones can carry weak content; a picture with interesting content but weak formal structure usually fails. This day isolates the formal skill so you can see it clearly.

## Shooting brief
Bold graphic opportunities: high-contrast shapes, strong diagonals, color fields, repeating geometry, silhouettes against plain backgrounds. The picture should hold even if the content is not immediately recognizable. Minimum 8 frames that succeed primarily on formal terms. Volume 40–70. Black-and-white thinking helps even if you shoot color.

## Technical & practical guidance
- Settings: Careful exposure for contrast. Protect highlights if you want deep blacks.
- Focusing: On the key graphic plane.
- Stance: Precise framing and edge control are essential.
- Light: Hard light or strong color contrast preferred.
- Distance: Variable — choose what best isolates the graphic structure.

Content can be minimal.

## Common failure modes
- Weak formal structure dressed up as “graphic.”
- Busy frames that lack a clear dominant shape, line, or contrast.
- Accepting content interest as a substitute for formal strength.
- Poor edge control.

## Good vs excellent
**Good:** Strong shapes or lines are present.  
**Excellent:** The formal arrangement is so clear and resolved that the picture holds power even if the subject is not immediately identifiable. Structure is the subject.
