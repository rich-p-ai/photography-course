# Day 05 – Waiting

**Core idea:** Choose a strong background or stage and commit to it until the right element enters.

## Why this exercise matters
Hunting produces quantity. Waiting produces pictures that feel inevitable because the stage was already resolved before the subject arrived. This is one of the highest-leverage skills in street photography and the foundation of much of Nick Turpin’s and Gustavo Minas’ strongest work. It also trains patience and reduces the anxiety of “needing” to shoot constantly.

## Shooting brief
Select 2–3 strong backgrounds (interesting wall, doorway, light patch, geometric corner, clean shadow area). Commit 10–15 minutes to each without leaving early. Photograph only when something worth including enters the frame. Minimum 5 frames that justify the wait. Volume will be lower (30–50) — that is expected.

## Technical & practical guidance
- Settings: Pre-set exposure for the background. Zone focus or single-point pre-focused on the expected subject plane (usually 3–6 meters).
- Focusing: Fixed zone or hyperfocal preferred so you do not have to hunt focus when the subject appears.
- Stance: Camera at ready height. Minimal movement once positioned. You become part of the furniture.
- Light: Choose backgrounds that already have good light. Do not wait in flat light.
- Distance: Fixed by the stage you chose.

Candid. The subject does not know they are walking into a prepared picture.

## Common failure modes
- Leaving after four or five minutes.
- Choosing weak backgrounds that cannot carry a picture.
- Shooting every person who passes instead of waiting for the right one.
- Fidgeting and changing position constantly.

## Good vs excellent
**Good:** Something entered a decent background.  
**Excellent:** The subject and the background complete each other so precisely that the picture looks pre-arranged. Timing and position feel inevitable.
