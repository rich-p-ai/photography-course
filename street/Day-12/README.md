# Day 12 – Reflections

**Core idea:** Use reflective surfaces to create layered or doubled realities and deliberately control the mix of reflection and direct view.

## Why this exercise matters
Reflections multiply information in a single frame and introduce controlled ambiguity. Mastery of them expands what one position can contain and is a major tool in the work of Saul Leiter, Vivian Maier, and Gustavo Minas. It also trains precise positioning — tiny changes in angle completely rewrite the content.

## Shooting brief
Windows, puddles, polished metal, mirrors, wet pavement. Decide whether the reflection, the reflected subject, or the interaction between both is primary. Minimum 6 intentional reflection frames. Volume 40–70. Try both clean and fragmented reflections.

## Technical & practical guidance
- Settings: Watch exposure carefully — reflective surfaces often trick the meter.
- Focusing: On the reflection plane or the key subject within the reflection.
- Stance: Tiny changes in angle and height completely change what is reflected. Work slowly.
- Light: Angle relative to the reflective surface is everything.
- Distance: Often closer work than usual.

Candid. Control or eliminate your own reflection unless it is a deliberate part of the picture.

## Common failure modes
- Uncontrolled self-reflection.
- Muddy mix of reflection and reality with no hierarchy.
- Weak or dirty reflective surfaces that add nothing.
- Focusing on the wrong plane so the key information is soft.

## Good vs excellent
**Good:** Reflection is used.  
**Excellent:** The reflection and the direct view create genuine visual tension or a new meaning that neither element has alone. The frame feels complex but resolved.
