# Day 11 – Shadows

**Core idea:** Make the shadow the primary subject or an equal partner to the object that casts it.

## Why this exercise matters
Shadows are pure graphic form and can function as secondary figures. Seeing them as content rather than byproduct changes how you use light and dramatically increases the number of usable frames in hard light. This is a core graphic skill shared by Cartier-Bresson, Erwitt, and many strong contemporary workers.

## Shooting brief
Hard light conditions (high sun or strong low-angle sun). Look for human shadows on walls or pavement, architectural shadow patterns, shadows that create secondary figures or abstract forms. Minimum 8 frames where the shadow is essential. Experiment with including and excluding the casting object. Volume 40–70.

## Technical & practical guidance
- Settings: Expose for the highlights if you want deep, graphic black shadows. Check the histogram.
- Focusing: On the shadow plane or the casting object depending on which is primary.
- Stance: Position relative to the light direction is critical. Small moves change the shadow dramatically.
- Light: Hard, directional light only. Soft light kills the exercise.
- Distance: Variable. Closer for abstract shadow forms; farther when the relationship between caster and shadow is the point.

Candid or pure shadow still life.

## Common failure modes
- Weak or soft shadows.
- Including too much of the casting object so the shadow becomes secondary.
- Busy ground or background that breaks the shadow shape.
- Exposing for the shadows and losing the graphic quality.

## Good vs excellent
**Good:** A strong shadow is present.  
**Excellent:** The shadow is more interesting or more complete than the object, or the two form a single graphic idea that neither has alone.
