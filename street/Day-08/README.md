# Day 08 – Looking Down

**Core idea:** Change viewpoint completely. Work the ground plane — feet, shadows, discarded objects, patterns, reflections in pavement.

## Why this exercise matters
Default eye-level is a habit, not a law. Many strong pictures exist only at different heights. Looking down forces new subject matter and breaks compositional autopilot. It also trains edge control and the ability to make complete frames from limited information.

## Shooting brief
Entire session looking downward. Subjects: feet and shoes, shadows, sidewalk textures, accidental compositions of litter, puddle reflections, etc. Minimum 8 intentional frames. Volume 40–60. Include both tight details and wider overhead views when possible (stairs, bridges, upper windows).

## Technical & practical guidance
- Settings: Higher shutter (1/320s+) to freeze hand movement while looking down.
- Focusing: Single-point or zone on the ground plane.
- Stance: Control your own shadow rigorously. It is the most common failure.
- Light: Hard light creates the best ground graphics and shadows.
- Distance: From waist height to true overhead.

Candid or pure still life of the street surface.

## Common failure modes
- Including your own feet or shadow by accident.
- Pure texture with no point or hierarchy.
- Weak edges and messy corners.
- Making the frame feel accidental rather than chosen.

## Good vs excellent
**Good:** Something interesting on the ground is recorded.  
**Excellent:** The downward view creates a complete, self-contained world that feels intentional. The frame has clear structure and would not work at eye level.
