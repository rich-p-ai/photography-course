# Day 06 – Following

**Core idea:** Stay with one moving subject for a short period and photograph the evolving relationship between the person and their environment.

## Why this exercise matters
It teaches sustained observation and the ability to build a small narrative through changing positions rather than isolated hits. It also forces you to manage distance, presence, and ethics in real time — skills that directly improve both candid street work and approached street portraits.

## Shooting brief
Choose one subject moving through public space. Follow discreetly for 5–12 minutes. Make a series showing different interactions with the surroundings. Stop immediately if the person notices and shows discomfort. Minimum coherent set of 6–8 frames. Volume 40–60.

## Technical & practical guidance
- Settings: AF-C, f/4–5.6, shutter 1/250s or faster.
- Focusing: Continuous on the subject.
- Stance: Parallel or slightly behind, never aggressive or predatory. Keep your body language neutral.
- Light: Work with what the path offers.
- Distance: Maintain respectful working distance (usually 4–10 meters). The longer end of the 24-50mm helps.

Primarily candid. If engagement happens, it ends the exercise for that subject.

## Common failure modes
- Getting too close too fast.
- Producing only similar frames from the same angle.
- Ignoring clear discomfort signals.
- Turning the follow into a chase.

## Good vs excellent
**Good:** A series of the same person in different places.  
**Excellent:** The series shows genuine change in the relationship between person and place, and the strongest single frame could stand alone with clear narrative weight.
