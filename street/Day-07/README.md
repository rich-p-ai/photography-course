# Day 07 – Behind

**Core idea:** Photograph people primarily from behind or strong three-quarter rear so posture, clothing, gesture, and context carry the picture.

## Why this exercise matters
It removes reliance on facial expression — the crutch of weak street photography. You are forced to read body language, clothing as character, and the relationship between figure and environment. These skills improve every picture that includes people, including approached street portraits.

## Shooting brief
Only rear or strong three-quarter rear angles. Minimum 8 frames where the absence of a full face is a strength, not a weakness. Volume 40–70. Dignity is non-negotiable.

## Technical & practical guidance
- Settings: f/4–5.6, shutter fast enough for gesture.
- Focusing: On the upper body or the strongest graphic element of the figure.
- Stance: Slightly lower or matching the subject’s direction of movement.
- Light: Backlight and side light are powerful for separation and silhouette.
- Distance: 2–6 meters.

Candid. Never make the person look mocked, diminished, or vulnerable.

## Common failure modes
- Weak or neutral posture.
- Cluttered backgrounds that compete with the figure.
- Making the person look small or exploited.
- Including just enough face that the constraint is broken without gaining expression.

## Good vs excellent
**Good:** No full face and the picture is still readable.  
**Excellent:** Posture, clothing, and environment create a complete character or story. The absence of the face feels like a deliberate strength.
